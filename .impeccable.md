## Design Context

### Users

Prospective customers, partners, collaborators, and technically curious product leaders use the xAGI Labs website to understand what the studio builds, how it approaches difficult product problems, and which products or experiments are ready to use, pilot, or discuss.

### Brand Personality

Inventive, grounded, and credible. The site should communicate independent product taste and technical depth without hype. It should feel confident enough for buyers and partners, transparent enough for technical readers, and human enough to reflect a small hands-on lab.

### Aesthetic Direction

An editorial studio casebook with strong typography, asymmetrical composition, and product-specific art direction. Shared navigation and page structure should make the work feel like one xAGI Labs portfolio, while color, illustration, and interaction details let each product retain its own identity. Support light and dark modes. Avoid generic SaaS card grids, neon AI gradients, decorative glassmorphism, inflated metrics, and invented testimonials.

### Design Principles

1. Lead with the real user problem and the product's point of view, then show how it works.
2. Separate shipped products, active prototypes, and research clearly; never turn a plan or local test into a launch claim.
3. Give each project a memorable visual identity inside a consistent editorial case-study system.
4. Prefer concrete capabilities, architecture, and verified product evidence over broad marketing language.
5. Keep every page responsive, keyboard accessible, high contrast, and respectful of reduced-motion preferences.
